Micro DC Gear Motor JGA25-370

JGA25-370 Micro DC Gear Motor Instruction Manual

Model: JGA25-370

1. Introduction

The JGA25-370 Micro DC Gear Motor is a versatile brushed DC motor featuring durable metal gears, adjustable speed capabilities, and support for both forward and reverse rotation. It is designed for various applications requiring controlled speed and high torque, operating across 6V, 12V, and 24V DC power supplies. This manual provides essential information for the safe and effective use of your motor.

Image 1: JGA25-370 Micro DC Gear Motor with key features: Metal gear, Adjustable speed, Forward and reverse rotation.

2. Safety Information

  • Always ensure the power supply voltage matches the motor's rated voltage (6V, 12V, or 24V) to prevent damage.
  • Avoid short-circuiting the motor terminals.
  • Do not exceed the rated current to prevent overheating and potential motor damage.
  • Keep the motor away from water, excessive dust, and corrosive environments.
  • Ensure proper ventilation during operation to dissipate heat.
  • Disconnect power before making any connections, adjustments, or maintenance.
  • The product contains small parts; keep out of reach of children.

3. Product Overview

The JGA25-370 is a compact DC gear motor designed for applications requiring precise speed and high torque. It consists of a DC brush motor coupled with a metal gearbox, providing various reduction ratios to achieve different output speeds. The motor supports bidirectional rotation by simply reversing the input voltage polarity.

5. Setup

5.1 Mounting

  • Secure the motor using the two M3 screw holes located on the front plate of the gearbox.
  • Ensure the mounting surface is flat and stable to prevent vibration and misalignment.
  • Refer to the dimensional drawing (Image 6) for precise mounting hole spacing (17mm).

5.2 Wiring

  • Connect your DC power supply to the motor terminals located at the rear of the motor.
  • The terminal marked with a red dot (or indicated as positive) should be connected to the positive (+) output of your power supply.
  • The other terminal should be connected to the negative (-) output.
  • To reverse the motor's direction of rotation, simply reverse the polarity of the power supply connections.

6. Operating Instructions

6.1 Power On/Off

  • Once properly wired, apply the appropriate DC voltage (6V, 12V, or 24V) to the motor terminals to start operation.
  • To stop the motor, disconnect the power supply.

6.2 Speed Control

  • The motor's output speed is primarily determined by the applied voltage and the selected internal reduction ratio.
  • For variable speed control, an external DC motor speed controller (e.g., a Pulse Width Modulation or PWM controller) can be used in conjunction with your power supply. This allows for fine-tuning the motor's RPM.

6.3 Direction Control

  • The JGA25-370 motor supports both forward and reverse rotation.
  • To change the direction of rotation, reverse the polarity of the DC power supply connected to the motor terminals.

7. Maintenance

  • Cleaning: Keep the motor clean and free from dust, dirt, and debris. Use a soft, dry cloth for cleaning.
  • Environment: Avoid exposing the motor to extreme temperatures, high humidity, or corrosive chemicals.
  • Lubrication: The gearbox is typically pre-lubricated and sealed. Do not attempt to lubricate the gears unless specifically instructed by the manufacturer or if the motor shows signs of wear requiring specialized maintenance.
  • Connections: Periodically check all electrical connections for tightness and signs of wear or corrosion.
  • Inspection: Visually inspect the motor and shaft for any physical damage or obstructions.

8. Troubleshooting

  • Motor not running:
    • Check power supply: Ensure the power supply is on, providing the correct voltage (6V, 12V, or 24V), and capable of supplying sufficient current.
    • Check wiring: Verify all connections are secure and correctly wired (positive to positive, negative to negative).
    • Check for obstructions: Ensure the motor shaft or gears are not physically jammed or blocked.
  • Motor running slowly or with reduced torque:
    • Check power supply: The voltage may be too low, or the power supply may not be providing enough current under load.
    • Check load: The motor may be overloaded. Reduce the mechanical load if possible.
    • Inspect for damage: Check for physical damage to the gears or motor windings.
  • Excessive noise or vibration:
    • Check mounting: Ensure the motor is securely mounted and all mounting screws are tightened.
    • Check for obstructions: Clear any foreign objects that might be interfering with the motor or gearbox.
    • Internal issue: If persistent, the motor may have internal damage to the gears or bearings and might require replacement.

9. User Tips

  • For precise and dynamic speed control, consider integrating a Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) controller with your DC power supply. This allows for smooth adjustment of motor speed without significant loss of torque.
  • When changing the direction of rotation, it is good practice to momentarily stop the motor before reversing the polarity. This reduces mechanical stress on the gears and extends the motor's lifespan.
  • Always test the motor with a light load first to confirm proper operation and wiring before applying it to its intended application with a full load.
  • If you notice any unusual heat, noise, or smell, immediately disconnect power and investigate the cause.
